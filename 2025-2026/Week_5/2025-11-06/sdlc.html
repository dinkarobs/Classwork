Software Development Life Cycle

The systems development life cycle (SDLC) describes the typical phases and progression between phases during the development of a computer-based system; from inception to retirement. At base, there is just one life cycle even though there are different ways to describe it; using differing numbers of and names for the phases. The SDLC is analogous to the life cycle of a living organism from its birth to its death. In particular, the SDLC varies by system in much the same way that each living organism has a unique path through its life.

Agile Description

A specific work cycle or stage within the overall agile project lifecycle, which emphasizes flexibility and iterative development

Agile Phases

Agile Phase Description
Concept The high-level stage of software development where the project's purpose, scope, and feasibility are determined before any actual coding begins
Inception The early stages of planning & envisioning how the finished product will look
Iteration A software development cycle that involves repeatedly building, testing, and refining a product in smaller, manageable steps
Release The stage of the software development life cycle (SDLC) where a finished, tested version of the software is made available to end-users
Maintenance The team fixes bugs, reviews customer issues & manages software changes
Retirement The final stage in the software development life cycle (SDLC). This phase involves the planned, systematic removal of a software system or application from production and active use.
